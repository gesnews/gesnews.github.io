One of Ours

What a Rock Looks Like Inside

By David Persaud

The rock laboratory has two new machines, funded by the department chair, Dr. J Bret Bennington: a Hi-Tech Diamond trim saw, which cuts a rock open, and an All-U-Need flat lap polisher, which smooths the cut face until the minerals show. David Persaud, a student in the department, has been using them.

The surface of a rock does not always tell you much, since years of weathering and erosion wear it down. Sawing a rock open reveals the true form of the rock: the minerals inside, grouped in patterns called assemblages that point to what kind of rock you are holding. Once you make the first cut, you keep sawing until the piece is around the size of a key, and then begin polishing. A polished face looks like a granite countertop: you can see every crystal, its shape, size and colour. That is where the rock finally tells you its story. For example, I am working on a piece of iron ore, which had a surface of dull greys and reds of indistinguishable minerals. Once I polished it, the face showed a bigger variety of minerals, and had a bright, metallic shine.

The saw and the polisher are in the rock laboratory in Gittleson Hall. Students working with a faculty mentor on an independent study or a research project can use them. Ask in your Geology, Environment and Sustainability course, or write to the department office.

My Summer with the Birds

By Renee Lista

Renee Lista, a graduate student in the department’s Sustainability program, spent her summer protecting shorebirds on Lido Beach.

From June through September, I interned with the Town of Hempstead Department of Conservation and Waterways, protecting endangered and threatened birds.

On a quad on Lido Beach, I spent my days monitoring piping plovers and black skimmers (our main focus, but I monitored all shorebirds), maintaining field logs and surveys, conducting banding resights, protecting beach habitat, engaging with the public, and supporting projects. These projects included bird banding, water sampling, invasive plant management, and the diamondback terrapin project.

The hardest part of the role was seeing how vicious a hungry bird can be; watching gulls prey on chicks was devastating. I left this internship with a new love for birds, months of hands-on wildlife conservation experience, and relationships I know I’ll carry with me for years to come.

Students interested in a 2027 seasonal position with the Town of Hempstead Department of Conservation and Waterways can send a letter of interest and a resume to Tara Schneider-Moran (CW-volunteers@hempsteadny.gov).

Write for GES-NEWS

GES-NEWS is looking for student contributing editors, whose names are printed at the top of every issue they work on.

What contributors write:

  • A first-person account of an internship, a field season, or a summer project
  • Your own research finding, written so that a first-year student understands it
  • A report from an event, a field trip or a conference, with photographs
  • Anything about student life in GES that other students would want to know

None of it is scientific writing.

“It is writing for people outside your subject, which is a separate skill and a useful one to have.”

To contribute a piece, or to join as a contributing editor for the year, write to the editor, antonios.marsellos@hofstra.edu, with GES-NEWS in the subject line.

How the dates are checked. Every application deadline in this issue was checked on the website of the organisation that offers it, and all of them were checked again on 12 October 2026. If an organisation has not posted next year’s deadline yet, as with the Lamont-Doherty summer internship, this newsletter says “not posted yet”. It does not print last year’s date, because that application is already closed. Some scholarships are not applied for directly: the university nominates you, so there is an internal Hofstra deadline months before the national one, as with the Goldwater Scholarship above.

GES-NEWS is published for students of the Department of Geology, Environment and Sustainability at Hofstra University, nine issues a year, September to May. If you find an error, write to the editor (antonios.marsellos@hofstra.edu) and it will be corrected in the next issue.
